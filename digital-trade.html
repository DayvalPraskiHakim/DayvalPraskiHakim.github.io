PPMLHDFE Bilateral Trade Data Governance

Digital Trade and Geopolitical Regime Divergence

A gravity-model test of whether digital regulatory divergence — specifically, government-imposed data localization — acts as a structural barrier to bilateral trade, independent of the usual gravity factors like distance, size, and shared borders.

Method
PPMLHDFE
Sample
6,960 bilateral pairs
Fixed Effects
Exporter-Year / Importer-Year / Pair
Toolchain
R

Key results

β = −0.34
Effect of data localization on bilateral trade flows
p < 0.05
0.98
Pseudo R², robust to pair fixed effects
6,960
Bilateral trade observations in the estimation sample

The question

Gravity models explain most of bilateral trade with distance, GDP, and shared history. This project asks what's left over once those are controlled for — specifically, whether a country's stance on cross-border data flows and government-imposed data localization requirements independently suppresses trade with its partners.

Methodology

Estimated via Pseudo-Poisson Maximum Likelihood with high-dimensional fixed effects (PPMLHDFE) — the standard for gravity estimation with zero trade flows and heteroskedastic errors. Exporter-year, importer-year, and pair fixed effects absorb multilateral resistance terms and any time-invariant bilateral factors, isolating the coefficient on digital regulatory divergence from confounding gravity effects.

Finding

Government-imposed data localization significantly reduces bilateral trade flows, and the effect survives the pair fixed-effects specification — meaning it isn't just proxying for some unobserved, time-invariant feature of the country pair. That points to restrictive data governance as a structural barrier to digital trade in its own right, not just a symptom of broader regulatory distance.
