Trade Policy Uncertainty and Export Volatility

A long-run and volatility-side look at how US trade policy uncertainty feeds into Indonesian exports — both the level effect on export volumes and the persistence of the volatility it creates.

Method
VECM-GARCH
Sample
128 monthly observations
Driver
US Trade Policy Uncertainty
Toolchain
R

Key results

β = −51.93
Long-run effect of a one-unit rise in US policy uncertainty on Indonesian exports
p < 0.001
1.68%
Monthly error-correction speed back toward the long-run equilibrium
α+β = 0.94
Near-IGARCH volatility persistence in export series

The question

Policy uncertainty is usually treated as a level shock — it lowers trade, then trade recovers. This project asks two separate questions: how large is the long-run level effect on Indonesian exports, and does the uncertainty itself leave a lasting mark on export volatility, independent of the level effect.

Methodology

A Vector Error Correction Model captures the long-run cointegrating relationship between US trade policy uncertainty and Indonesian export volumes across 128 monthly observations, with a GARCH specification layered on the residuals to characterize volatility persistence separately from the level relationship. Impulse response functions trace how long a given uncertainty shock takes to work through the system.

Findings

A one-unit rise in US policy uncertainty reduces Indonesian exports by roughly 52 units in the long run, with the effect persisting through 8 or more periods according to the impulse response analysis. The error-correction term is slow — about 1.68% of the deviation closes per month — meaning the system takes a long time to return to equilibrium after a shock. On the volatility side, the sum of the GARCH parameters sits close to the unit-root (IGARCH) boundary, indicating that policy uncertainty shocks create a volatility regime that doesn't quickly normalize — a "sticky" risk climate rather than a transient spike.
