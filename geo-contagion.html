Macroeconomic Shocks and Digital Lending

A Panel Local Projections model measuring the dynamic, time-series impact of macroeconomic shocks on digital loan default rates — translating econometric output into a structured risk read for regulatory and institutional use.

The question

Digital lending platforms sit closer to the macroeconomic cycle than traditional credit — shorter loan terms, thinner underwriting, and borrowers with less buffer against income shocks. This project asks how macroeconomic shocks actually propagate into default rates over time, rather than assuming a simple contemporaneous relationship.

Methodology

A Panel Local Projections framework traces the full dynamic response of digital loan default rates to a macroeconomic shock, period by period, rather than imposing the fixed lag structure a VAR would require. The panel structure pools across lending segments to gain statistical power while local projections keep the impulse-response estimation flexible at each horizon.

Why it matters

Understanding the dynamic path of default risk — not just its eventual level — gives regulators and lenders a usable early-warning window rather than a single static estimate. The end goal is a structured risk assessment that translates the local-projection impulse responses into something institutional risk teams can act on directly.
