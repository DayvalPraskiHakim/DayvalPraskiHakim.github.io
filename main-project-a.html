Geopolitical Risk Transmission and Capital Flight in EMDE Currencies: A Hawkes Process Approach

A self-exciting jump-diffusion framework for how geopolitical shocks trigger cascading capital flight across emerging-market and developing-economy currencies. The central result is a bifurcation: past a critical excitation threshold, contagion stops behaving like a one-off jump and starts behaving like a self-sustaining chain of events, one shock quietly setting off the next.

Pre-submission

The question

Capital flight from emerging markets is usually modeled as a reaction to a single shock. That misses something important: geopolitical stress tends to arrive in clusters, and each event changes the probability of the next one. This paper treats currency crashes as a self-exciting point process, where a jump doesn't just move the exchange rate — it temporarily raises the intensity of future jumps.

The goal is to identify when that self-excitation crosses a threshold and contagion becomes self-sustaining, independent of any further external shock — a structural bifurcation point rather than a matter of degree.

Methodology

Currency crash classification is disentangled from tail direction and scrubbed for FOMC and NFP announcement windows to isolate geopolitical from monetary-policy-driven jumps — orthogonalizing the process along what the paper refers to internally as Path B. The excitation parameter is reparametrized and bounded to guarantee stationarity of the fitted process, rather than checking stationarity post hoc.

  • Self-exciting Hawkes process fit via bounded maximum likelihood, with the self-excitation parameter constrained to enforce stationarity by construction
  • Crash classification separated from tail direction to avoid conflating one- and two-tailed events
  • FOMC and NFP announcement windows scrubbed to isolate geopolitical from monetary-policy shocks
  • Five robustness tables recomputed under the corrected specification
